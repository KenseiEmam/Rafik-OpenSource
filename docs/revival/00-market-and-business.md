# Market research and business decision

**Researched:** 22 September 2026. **Evidence:** founder account, local product audit, and current public first-party product/help/pricing pages. This is a decision-oriented desk study, not customer interviews, a market-size forecast, or hands-on comparative testing of paid competitor accounts.

## 1. The diagnosis changes after the founder's account

The founder reports that independent coaches expressed interest but did not adopt; people downloaded the app but did not meaningfully use it; and an organizational pivot reached introductory meetings without customers. The founder suspects lengthy signup, learning effort, and no immediate reward. Coaches invited to beta-test did not receive new clients because Rafik had no audience. A few programs and a coach existed for clients, but the experience still felt effortful.

This describes **failure to activate**, not yet a proven retention problem. A person who never successfully starts has not demonstrated whether the recurring product is valuable. There are at least four separate hypotheses:

| Hypothesis | Evidence now | What would distinguish it |
|---|---|---|
| Friction prevents an otherwise useful first session | Founder report plus mandatory setup/route/save issues | A much shorter working flow produces actual starts and repeat sessions |
| Coaches expected customer acquisition, not client-management software | Founder reports no new-client reward for beta coaches | Ask what result they expected and whether they already have clients to serve |
| The product is not materially better than existing habits/tools | No adoption despite interest; established alternatives exist | Observe current workflow and compare time/error outcomes on the same task |
| Targeting/sales lacked a compelling owner, budget or urgent problem | Introductory organization meetings did not convert | Named operator and buyer agree a small pilot, start date and payment decision |

Do not assume signup is the only cause. A shorter signup can expose an undifferentiated product faster. Conversely, absence of customers does not prove fitness demand is absent; it proves this offer and acquisition process have not converted.

**Research conclusion:** there is no defensible basis for a general “all-in-one fitness app for the region” relaunch. Rafik should compete on one demonstrated improvement for one reachable group, and earn expansion through repeat use.

## 3. Market context does not establish a business case

Dubai's official media office reported more than three million participants in the 2025 Dubai Fitness Challenge. This supports the existence of substantial local fitness engagement. It does **not** measure unique paying app customers, coach-software demand, or Rafik's reachable market. Do not turn the event count into a TAM calculation. [Dubai Media Office, 12 December 2025](https://cd1.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/december/12-12/dubai-fitness-challenge).

RevenueCat's 2026 report analyzes more than 115,000 apps using its subscription infrastructure. It reports lower 12-month subscriber retention for AI apps than non-AI apps across the compared plan durations. The sample is not a census of UAE fitness apps, and the comparison is observational. It does not show that adding or removing AI causes a particular result for Rafik. Use it as a reason to measure lasting value, not as a forecast or justification for an aggressive paywall. [Report and methodology](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps).

No credible bottom-up reachable-customer count, Rafik acquisition cost, willingness-to-pay estimate, or unit economics is available yet. Estimating those requires an actual list of reachable qualified buyers and observed conversion, not a broad fitness-industry growth statistic.

## 4. Compare the business models before choosing the product

| Model | First user benefit | Who pays and why | Acquisition dependency | Main risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coach/client marketplace | A relevant coach or a qualified lead | Commission/lead fee on successful matching | Must attract both sides and build trust/matching quality | Repeats the exact no-users/no-leads problem; service operations and economics unproven | Do not revive as the primary model |
| Broad coach SaaS | Manage existing clients more efficiently | Coach pays for time saved/service quality | Reach coaches who already have paying clients | Mature/free alternatives and switching effort | Reject broad scope; test one painful delivery task |
| Enterprise/school platform | Team oversight and measurable operating outcome | Organization budget holder | Operator champion, decision-maker, budget, procurement and rollout | Meetings can continue without commitment; custom features expand scope | Defer unless a buyer commits to a bounded paid pilot |
| General consumer subscription | A useful workout/plan immediately | Individual pays for continued guidance/progression | Direct content/search/community/paid distribution | Free alternatives and costly discovery; downloads do not prove habits | Test a narrow offer only; do not build a full second product |
| **Existing-plan workout companion** | **Client opens a familiar plan and can train; coach sees useful results without rebuilding their business** | **Initially coach/team pays if it saves time and clients use it** | **One coach supplies both a plan and existing client distribution** | **Still competes with Hevy/Everfit; must prove a concrete advantage** | **Recommended first experiment, conditional on real commitments** |
| Manual coaching/onboarding service supported by software | Human setup and accountability | Customer pays for the service | Founder/coach capacity and trusted relationships | Labor scales with revenue; different business from software | Acceptable if evidence favors it, but price and operate it as a service |

The recommendation is the **lowest-cost credible experiment given the evidence**, not a proven highest-probability business. It differs from the old coach beta by explicitly excluding lead generation, doing the initial plan setup for the coach, using their real clients, removing install/profile work before preview, and asking for a paid continuation decision.

If qualified coaches only want leads, do not rename a SaaS dashboard to satisfy them. Either validate a manual matching service separately or stop that segment. If nobody values the narrower delivery benefit enough to switch or pay, a full rebuild is not justified.

## 5. The initial offer and immediate reward

**Candidate offer to coaches:** “Send the training plan you already use. We will set up a client-ready workout link. Your client can follow it without learning a complicated app, and you can see the result.” This is proposed test copy, not a claim that an import feature already exists.

**Candidate offer to clients:** “Your next workout is ready. See each exercise, record your sets, and know what to do next.” The reward is a useful decision and a usable session, not points, badges, or a promise of future physical transformation.

Start with a narrow group such as adult beginner/returning gym clients of an independent coach who currently sends simple strength plans. Confirm the actual task in interviews. Keep the choice narrow enough that one small exercise library and prescription model can serve it.

**Before account creation:** show a shareable, non-personalized routine preview with duration/equipment, exercise examples and a clear start action. A coach may explicitly publish a reusable routine; private health information, personal notes, individualized prescriptions and progress remain protected. An invitation landing page can identify the public coach without revealing private client details.

**Before a coach learns a builder:** manually import their first plan and show the client view. Record the labor required. Automate import only when enough similar plans justify it; do not build an AI document parser as a prerequisite.

**During the first workout:** easy set entry, sensible units and recoverable progress. **On return:** previous performance and the next workout are ready. **For the coach:** a meaningful completion or question, not another dashboard to maintain.

A public sample/guest session can run with local persistence. Offer account creation when the user wants backup, cross-device history or connection to the coach, with a single clear reason. Personalized assignment access still needs verified identity. Do not turn delayed registration into accidental public exposure of private programs.

NN/g's usability research describes login walls as barriers before people understand a site's value. This supports testing a preview-first flow; it does not quantify Rafik's expected conversion uplift. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/login-walls/).

## 6. Distribution and positioning

The first channel is existing coach-to-client communication: the coach chooses to share a routine/invitation link through the channel they already use. Rafik should not require moving every conversation into its own chat before it provides value. This audit has not contacted or messaged anyone.

Recruit operators with a real weekly task and a roster they control. For an organization, qualify four items before custom work: named operator, budget holder, a measurable problem, and a pilot start/payment decision. A positive meeting without those is a lead, not validation.

Avoid “beta-test our platform” as the central proposition. Offer to solve one current delivery problem. Avoid claiming Rafik will bring clients unless it is running and measuring an actual acquisition service. Avoid paid advertising until organic/partner-distributed users reach first value and return.

For consumer demand, run a small alternative offer using the same non-private starter session: “A clear first gym workout in Arabic or English.” Compare its actual use and willingness to continue with the coach-distributed route. This is one bounded test, not permission to add nutrition, social feeds or a second roadmap.

## 7. Bounded discovery and validation campaign

The sequence is analysis → core-flow design → working slice → observed use. Completing the design audit and fixing established usability defects does not depend on recruiting customers first. Interviews and offer tests guide the scope of the investment; they are not a substitute for rebuilding the experience. The initial discovery can fit roughly two weeks; actual repeat-use and payment evidence require additional time after the slice works.

All thresholds below are proposed decision rules, not statistical benchmarks. Small samples identify failure modes; they do not establish market share or precise conversion rates. Report counts, recruitment source and assistance given.

| Experiment | Method | Evidence required | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| X1: reconstruct the failed beta | Interview 5 former interested coaches, 5 downloaders and up to 3 organization contacts if reachable; inspect their last attempted task and current alternative | Distinguish wanted-leads, wanted-software, unusable-flow, no-urgent-need and timing/budget causes | Choose a pain based on concrete behavior, not compliments |
| X2: actual coach commitment | Recruit up to 10 qualified coaches with existing clients; offer manual setup of one current plan | At least 3 supply a real plan and nominate at least 3 real clients each, with an agreed pilot date and future price discussion | If fewer commit, revise the offer/segment before expanding beyond the useful core; do not rebuild the old platform breadth |
| X3: immediate-value usability | Test preview → start → log → resume with 5 clients, including Arabic-first users where relevant; use a prototype first and a working thin slice next | At least 4/5 find their next action unaided; median time to usable preview under 60 seconds; no lost entries in the interruption scenario | Fix the observed barriers before recruiting more users |
| X4: repeated usefulness | Over 14 days, observe at least 10 eligible clients on the working thin slice; count actual sessions, not app opens | At least 6/10 complete a second session on a different day without individual founder chasing; record coach prompts separately | Failure means the benefit/habit remains unproven, even if signup succeeds |
| X5: willingness to pay | Give 3 engaged coaches a specific continuation offer after real usage; no hypothetical “would you pay?” question alone | At least 2 pay/authorize an agreed paid pilot or sign a dated paid continuation; track who actually pays | Free interest alone does not pass |
| X6: compare a direct-client offer | Recruit a small clearly separate sample for a guest starter workout using the same thin slice; no large ad budget | First workout, second-session return, reason to choose over current alternative, actual paid-continuation response | Change the model only if this evidence is stronger; do not combine incompatible cohorts |

Do not compress X4/X5 observation to meet an engineering calendar. Codex will prepare the corrected product, scripts, offers and measurement; actual outreach and participant availability remain separate inputs. These observations guide product direction and expansion, not whether established UI/UX problems are fixed. The implementation plan does not assume a staffed team or a ten-engineer-day budget.

The founder has clarified that the old product is inactive and its data does not need migration. This materially lowers revival cost: build the new core locally, using a new Supabase project when hosting is needed, without restoring legacy accounts, credits or native compatibility. Keep mobile work until the web/backend is ready.

## 8. Pricing and economics

Use one understandable pilot offer with the service scope and payment date spelled out. Keep clients free initially under coach-paid delivery; do not charge both sides before either sees value. Do not copy competitor prices mechanically. Test price against observed saved time, customer budget and actual recurring use.

Measure these quantities per cohort:

- **Monthly contribution:** cash revenue minus payment/store fees, infrastructure, provider/media cost and support/onboarding labor valued at a stated rate.
- **Acquisition cost:** attributable channel spend plus recruiting/sales labor divided by new paying accounts; separately report unpaid founder effort.
- **Payback:** acquisition cost divided by positive monthly contribution per account. Do not calculate it when contribution is negative or retention is unknown.
- **Setup burden:** minutes to map/import a coach plan and invite their first clients; recurring support minutes per active coach.
- **Continuation:** actual paid renewal and retained weekly use, with cohort size and observation window.

Do not extrapolate lifetime value from a four-week cohort. If manual imports/support erase the value of a low-priced subscription, simplify the supported plan format, charge for setup, or acknowledge that the viable model is a service. Existing data does not support a revenue forecast.

## 9. Decision at the end of validation

**Proceed** with the broader delivery and reliability work for the narrow workout-delivery product when real coaches bring real clients, clients return, and someone pays for the demonstrated benefit. Fund the next stage of the technical plan, rather than restoring all the old scope.

**Revise** when a specific friction point prevents an otherwise desired action: fix it and repeat with a new small sample. A second vague-interest-only result is not a signal to add features.

**Change model** only if another tested segment supplies stronger behavior and payment evidence. An institution can become the buyer when it funds a bounded outcome; consumer-first can win if standalone use and distribution work without a coach.

**Stop a full revival** if qualified prospects do not commit, repeat use remains absent after a targeted correction, or the support economics cannot work. Preserve the reusable assets and findings. The best way to improve the chance of success includes avoiding another expensive build whose business premise has not been tested.

## 10. What the live data adds, and what it cannot answer

The live audit found 141 completed non-rest workout sheets across 11 client IDs, with the latest populated completion timestamp in February 2026. There were 2,182 pending follow-up forms and 2,182 form-linked messages out of 2,213 total messages. Test records, automation and incomplete timestamps limit commercial interpretation. This is not a valid count of acquired customers or a retention cohort.

It supports the founder's report of inactivity and shows why rows created/messages sent must not be the new engagement metric. It does not prove which UI screen caused abandonment or establish a paying market. Those require observing the corrected task and a concrete continuation offer.
